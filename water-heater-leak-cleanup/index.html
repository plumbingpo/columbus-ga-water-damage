Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Columbus: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Columbus crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Platform and Subfloor

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Shared Garage Wall

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether flooring in adjoining rooms was reached and what type it is.
  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.
  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.

Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
